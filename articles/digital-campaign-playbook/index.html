Inside our playbook for always-on digital campaigns

Digital Team Nov 20, 2026 4 min read

Most campaigns still get planned like fireworks: one big launch, a spike, then silence. Here's why we plan Digital's work more like a conversation that never fully stops.

#Digital #Strategy

When a campaign is treated as a single event, everything gets compressed into the week before launch — and everything goes quiet the week after. The budget spikes, the creative team sprints, and then the account goes dark until the next brief. It looks busy. It isn't always effective.

Our Digital team plans the other way around: a steady baseline of content and paid activity that runs continuously, with planned spikes layered on top for real moments — a product drop, an event, a partnership. The baseline keeps an audience warm; the spikes give it something to react to.

Why "always-on" beats the campaign burst

A channel that goes quiet between campaigns loses the algorithmic trust and audience habit it spent weeks building. Platforms reward consistency, and audiences do too — a page that posts reliably earns more attention per post than one that disappears for a month and then shouts.

"The best-performing push is rarely the loudest one. It's the one that lands on an audience that was already listening."

In practice this means a lighter, faster production rhythm for everyday content, reserving the bigger production budget — and the Production team's time — for the moments that actually deserve a bigger swing. Measurement runs the same way: weekly check-ins on what's working, not a single report filed after the campaign has already ended.
