Turning noise into narrative: how we think about PR

PR Team Oct 22, 2026 4 min read

A press release is easy to write. Deciding whether this is even the moment to say something — that's the actual job.

#PR #Media

Every brand has more to say than it should actually say out loud this week. Part of PR's job is deciding what gets held back — not because it isn't true, but because a story told at the wrong moment competes with itself for attention.

Before anything goes to a journalist or a partner, we ask what the headline a reader would write themselves looks like. If we can't guess it in one sentence, the pitch usually isn't ready yet, no matter how much detail is in the draft.

Timing is a creative decision, not just a calendar one

News cycles move fast enough that a genuinely good story can still land flat if it's released into a week already full of louder headlines. Part of the PR team's work is simply patience — holding a story that's ready, until the week it actually has room to breathe.

"A story that's true but told at the wrong time reads the same to an audience as a story that isn't true at all: it gets ignored."

That's also why PR sits close to Digital and Design on bigger moments — the press narrative, the social rollout, and the visual identity around a launch all need to agree with each other, or the whole thing reads as three separate announcements instead of one.
