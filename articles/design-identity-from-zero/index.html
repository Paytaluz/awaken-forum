Building a visual identity from a blank page

Design Team Nov 14, 2026 5 min read

Every identity project starts the same way: no logo, no colors, no rules — just a brand that needs to become recognizable. Here's the order we actually work in.

#Design #Brand

A logo is usually the last thing we draw, not the first. Before any shape gets fixed, the Design team spends time on words: three or four adjectives the brand should earn in someone's head after one look. Confident or approachable, serious or playful — those words decide more of the outcome than any font choice does.

Only after that do we move into mood boards and rough type pairings, deliberately looking at more references than we'll ever use, just to map the space before narrowing in. It's slower at the start and much faster by the end, because fewer decisions get revisited later.

Rules before assets

The deliverable a client actually lives with for years isn't the logo file — it's the rulebook around it. Clear spacing, a locked color system, type pairings that survive a tired intern formatting a slide at 11pm. An identity that only looks good in the one mockup it was designed for isn't finished yet.

"If the brand only works in the exact file we designed it in, we haven't designed a system — we've designed one poster."

That's also why Design and Production stay in the same room early. A mark that looks sharp on screen can fall apart printed two meters wide on a banner, or animated at 24 frames a second. Catching that in week one is a five-minute fix. Catching it after sign-off is a reprint.
